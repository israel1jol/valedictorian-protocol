Core engine

The Daily Loop

Run every study day, in order

Same loop every day, regardless of subject. The order matters: you encode, immediately test, lock in what leaked, then prove you can explain it. Step 5 is where English fluency is actually built.

STEP 1

Learn

Khan Academy unit videos + reading for the day's topic. No note-taking marathons — watch, pause, predict.

~40 min
STEP 2

Drill

Unit practice problems immediately after. Wrong answers are the curriculum — review every miss.

~30 min
STEP 3

Card

Anki cards for every miss and every looked-up fact. Then clear the day's review queue. Non-negotiable.

~15 min
STEP 4

Teach

Explain today's concept out loud in 60 seconds, no notes, like you're teaching it. If you stumble, you don't know it yet.

~5 min
STEP 5

English

Read above your level (20–30 min) + write one tight summary paragraph of what you learned today.

~30 min
Phase 1

Chemistry

Weeks 01–07 · AP Chemistry standard

Why first: chemistry is the bridge subject. Physics needs math you already have; biology leans on chemistry constantly. Everything here compounds — skip nothing.

WEEK01

Atomic Structure & Periodicity

Atoms, isotopes, ions · electron configuration & orbitals · photoelectron spectroscopy basics · periodic trends: atomic radius, ionization energy, electronegativity.

Drill — Khan AP Chem Unit 1 quizzes + unit test. Teach-back: explain why ionization energy rises across a period.

WEEK02

Bonding & Molecular Geometry

Ionic vs. covalent vs. metallic bonding · Lewis structures · VSEPR shapes · bond polarity & dipoles · intermolecular forces (London, dipole-dipole, H-bonding).

Drill — Draw Lewis structures cold for 20 molecules; predict shape + polarity. Khan Units 2–3.

WEEK03

Stoichiometry

The mole & Avogadro · balancing equations · limiting reagent · percent yield · empirical & molecular formulas · reaction types.

Drill — This is the math-heavy week; it plays to your strengths. 30+ stoich problems until conversions are reflexive. Khan Unit 4.

WEEK04

Gases & Solutions

Ideal gas law & kinetic molecular theory · partial pressures · molarity & dilutions · solubility · colligative properties overview.

Drill — PV=nRT problem set; mixed dilution calculations. Teach-back: why does a balloon shrink in cold air, at the particle level?

WEEK05

Thermodynamics & Kinetics

Enthalpy, entropy, Gibbs free energy · Hess's law · calorimetry · rate laws & reaction order · activation energy & catalysts.

Drill — Khan Units 5–6. Predict spontaneity from ΔG for 10 reactions; derive rate laws from data tables.

WEEK06

Equilibrium

Dynamic equilibrium · Keq expressions · ICE tables · Le Chatelier's principle · reaction quotient Q vs. K.

Drill — ICE table problems until automatic. Teach-back: explain Le Chatelier using a real industrial example (Haber process).

WEEK07

Acids, Bases & Electrochemistry

pH & pOH · strong vs. weak acids · titrations & buffers · redox reactions · galvanic cells & cell potential.

Drill — Titration curve interpretation; balance redox in acidic/basic solution. Khan Units 7–9 highlights.

End-of-phase boss fight — Take a full released AP Chemistry exam, timed. Target: 4–5 equivalent. Every miss becomes an Anki card before Phase 2 starts.
Phase 2

Physics

Weeks 08–12 · AP Physics 1 + select 2

Compressed to 5 weeks because your math and CS background carry most of the load — and half of this is formalizing intuitions you already chase recreationally.

WEEK08

Kinematics & Dynamics

Vectors & motion graphs · constant acceleration equations · projectile motion · Newton's three laws · friction & inclined planes · free-body diagrams.

Drill — Free-body diagram for every problem, no exceptions. 25 mixed kinematics/dynamics problems.

WEEK09

Energy, Momentum & Gravitation

Work-energy theorem · conservation of energy · momentum & impulse · elastic vs. inelastic collisions · uniform circular motion · universal gravitation & orbits.

Drill — Solve the same collision problems via momentum AND energy; know when each applies. Teach-back: why astronauts float (it's not "no gravity").

WEEK10

Rotation, Oscillations & Waves

Torque & angular momentum · rotational inertia · simple harmonic motion · wave properties · sound & Doppler · superposition, interference & diffraction.

Drill — Your single-slit question lives here — now derive it properly. Standing wave and interference pattern problems.

WEEK11

Electricity & Magnetism

Electric fields & Coulomb's law · circuits: Ohm's law, series/parallel, Kirchhoff · capacitors basics · magnetic fields & forces · electromagnetic induction.

Drill — Build/analyze 15 circuit problems. Teach-back: explain how a generator works using induction, in 60 seconds.

WEEK12

Thermo & Modern Physics

Heat transfer & thermodynamic laws · gas laws revisited (chem callback) · photoelectric effect · wave-particle duality, formalized · atomic models & nuclear basics.

Drill — Photoelectric effect problem set; connect E=hf to your existing duality intuitions.

End-of-phase boss fight — Full released AP Physics 1 exam, timed, plus selected Physics 2 free-response on E&M and modern physics. Misses → Anki.
Phase 3

Biology

Weeks 13–20 · AP Biology standard

Last and longest — it's the most memorization-heavy, so Anki carries the most weight here. Treat the cell like system architecture: mechanisms and data flow, not vocabulary lists.

WEEK13

Chemistry of Life

Water's properties · carbon & functional groups · macromolecules: carbs, lipids, proteins, nucleic acids · enzyme structure, function & inhibition.

Drill — Direct payoff from Phase 1. Map each macromolecule: monomer → polymer → function. Khan AP Bio Unit 1.

WEEK14

Cells & Membranes

Prokaryote vs. eukaryote · organelles & their jobs · membrane structure · passive vs. active transport · osmosis & tonicity · surface-area-to-volume.

Drill — Diagram a cell as a system architecture: each organelle is a service, label its API. Khan Unit 2.

WEEK15

Cellular Energetics

ATP as energy currency · glycolysis, Krebs cycle, electron transport chain · fermentation · photosynthesis: light reactions & Calvin cycle.

Drill — Trace one glucose molecule end-to-end out loud. Teach-back: why do we breathe oxygen, mechanistically?

WEEK16

Cell Cycle & Communication

Cell signaling pathways · feedback loops (positive/negative) · the cell cycle & checkpoints · mitosis · what cancer is, mechanistically.

Drill — Map a signal transduction pathway like an event-driven system. Khan Units 3–4.

WEEK17

Heredity

Meiosis & genetic variation · Mendel's laws · Punnett squares, mono & dihybrid · non-Mendelian: linkage, sex-linked traits, incomplete dominance · chromosomal disorders.

Drill — 20 genetics problems; treat Punnett squares as probability trees (they are). Khan Unit 5.

WEEK18

Molecular Genetics

DNA structure & replication · transcription & translation · gene regulation (operons, epigenetics intro) · mutations · biotech: PCR, gel electrophoresis, CRISPR overview.

Drill — DNA → RNA → protein is literally a compiler pipeline; explain it that way, then in proper bio terms. Khan Unit 6.

WEEK19

Evolution

Natural selection & fitness · evidence for evolution · Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium · genetic drift & gene flow · speciation · phylogenetic trees.

Drill — Hardy-Weinberg math problems (your stats instincts apply). Read tree diagrams cold. Khan Unit 7.

WEEK20

Ecology & Physiology

Energy flow & trophic levels · population growth models · community interactions · biogeochemical cycles · human body systems overview: nervous, circulatory, immune, endocrine.

Drill — Logistic vs. exponential growth (you know these curves from Big-O). Khan Unit 8 + physiology survey.

End-of-phase boss fight — Full released AP Biology exam, timed. The free-response sections double as English writing assessment: clear, mechanistic explanations under time pressure.
Parallel track

English

Daily · all 24 weeks · ~30 min

English isn't a phase, it's the operating system. Reading rebuilds vocabulary and sentence rhythm; the teach-back drill builds spoken fluency; daily writing makes both permanent. This also directly attacks the "narrate your reasoning aloud" gap from your interview prep — double duty.

Read

Daily · 20–30 min

Writing above your current level: rotate The Atlantic, The Economist, and Paul Graham essays. Plus one novel at a time from the ladder below. Look up every unfamiliar word — straight into Anki.

Speak

Daily · the teach-back

Step 4 of the daily loop is your speaking rep: 60 seconds, no notes, teaching today's concept to an imaginary student. Precision under time pressure is the skill.

Write

Daily · 1 paragraph

One tight paragraph summarizing what you learned. No filler words, no hedging. Strong writing and strong speaking are the same skill at different speeds.

Record & Review

Weekly · 3 min

Record yourself explaining the week's hardest concept for 3 minutes. Listen back. Painful, but it's the fastest feedback loop that exists. Note two fixes for next week.

Phase 4

Synthesis

Weeks 21–24 · The final boss

Knowing three subjects separately is a senior. Connecting them is a valedictorian. This phase converts retained facts into the kind of cross-domain fluency that makes you sound like you actually understand things — because you will.

WEEK21

Weak-Point Sweep I

Your Anki stats are the map — sort by lowest retention, attack the worst 20%. Retake the lowest-scoring sections from each phase exam.

Drill — No new content. Pure repair. Rewrite leaky Anki cards so they actually stick.

WEEK22

Weak-Point Sweep II

Second pass on remaining weak units. Mixed-subject problem sets — interleaving forces real recall instead of context-cued recall.

Drill — Random topic generator: pull any concept from the 20 weeks, explain it cold in 60 seconds.

WEEK23

Cross-Discipline Synthesis

Explain topics that need all three subjects: why ATP stores energy (chem + bio) · how nerve signals propagate (physics + bio) · why hemoglobin binds oxygen (all three) · how greenhouse gases work (chem + physics + ecology).

Drill — Write one 300-word essay per topic. This is your English final, disguised as science.

WEEK24

Final Boss

Fresh released exams for all three sciences, full timed conditions, across the week. Plus one recorded 5-minute talk: explain a topic of your choice to a non-technical audience.

Drill — Target: 4–5 equivalent on every exam. That's the objective definition of "valedictorian level." Hit it and you're done.

Non-Negotiable Rules

  • 01Never re-watch or re-read passively. If you're reviewing, you're testing yourself. Recall is the only thing that counts.
  • 02The Anki queue gets cleared every single day, including rest days. 15 minutes protects months of work.
  • 03If the 60-second teach-back stumbles, the concept goes back on tomorrow's list. Fluency is the bar, not familiarity.
  • 04Wrong answers are the curriculum. Every miss becomes a card the same day, or it's a miss you'll repeat.
  • 05Falling behind? Cut scope, not the loop. A shallow week with all five steps beats a deep week with two.
  • 06Validation is external: released AP exams, timed, scored honestly. No vibes-based progress assessment.
